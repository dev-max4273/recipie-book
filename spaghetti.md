**Core Principle**  
Bolognese is not a tomato sauce with meat. It is a meat-based sauce where tomatoes are a condiment. Your primary levers are **fat emulsion**, **umami stacking**, and **time**.  

**The 80/20 Ingredient Framework**  
*Function over list.*  
- **Protein/Fat**: 500g ground beef (20% fat), 250g ground pork. (Fat carries flavor; lean meat fails).  
- **Soffritto (Foundation)**: 1 medium onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk. Finely minced. Equal parts.  
- **Umami/Acid**: 2 tbsp tomato paste, 400g canned crushed tomatoes, ½ cup whole milk, ½ cup dry wine (red or white).  
- **Force Multipliers**: 1 Parmesan rind, 1 tsp anchovy paste (dissolves completely, adds zero fish taste, pure umami), salt, black pepper.  

**Execution Algorithm**  
1. **Sweat, Don’t Burn**: Heat 2 tbsp olive oil + 1 tbsp butter over medium-low. Add soffritto. Cook 10–15 mins until soft and sweet. Do not brown.  
2. **Sear & Render**: Increase heat to medium-high. Add meat. Break it up. Cook until all moisture evaporates and the meat begins to fry in its own fat (critical for depth).  
3. **Caramelize & Deglaze**: Push meat aside. Add tomato paste and anchovy paste. Cook 2 mins until brick-red. Pour in wine. Scrape the pan. Reduce to completely dry.  
4. **Emulsify**: Pour in milk. Simmer until fully absorbed. (This neutralizes tomato acidity and tenderizes the meat).  
5. **Simmer**: Add crushed tomatoes, Parmesan rind, salt, and pepper. Drop heat to the lowest setting. Partially cover. Simmer **minimum 2 hours** (3–4 is optimal). Stir occasionally. Add a splash of water if it binds too tightly.  

**Integration (The Final Lever)**  
Do not pour sauce over dry pasta.  
1. Boil spaghetti in heavily salted water. Undercook by 2 minutes.  
2. Transfer pasta directly into the Bolognese with ½ cup of starchy pasta water.  
3. Agitate vigorously over medium heat for 2 minutes. The starch and fat will emulsify into a cohesive, glossy coating.  

**Failure Points to Avoid**  
- Rushing the soffritto.  
- Skipping the milk.  
- Using lean meat.  
- Serving immediately. (It tastes better on day two. Make it ahead.)
