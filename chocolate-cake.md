**Core Principle**  
A superior chocolate cake is an exercise in chemistry, not sentiment. Your primary levers are **blooming the cocoa**, **oil-driven moisture retention**, and **acid-base leavening**.  

**The 80/20 Ingredient Framework**  
*Function over list.*  
- **Structure & Hygroscopy**: 200g all-purpose flour, 400g granulated sugar. (Do not reduce sugar; it retains moisture and tenderizes).  
- **Flavor Base**: 75g high-quality Dutch-processed cocoa powder.  
- **Leavening**: 1.5 tsp baking powder, 1.5 tsp baking soda, 1 tsp fine salt.  
- **Fat & Acid**: 2 large eggs, 240ml buttermilk, 120ml neutral oil (canola or grapeseed).  
- **The Force Multiplier**: 240ml boiling water or hot black coffee. (Blooms the cocoa, unlocking depth that butter cannot achieve).  

**Execution Algorithm**  
1. **Prep**: Heat oven to 175°C (350°F). Grease and line two 20cm (8-inch) round pans with parchment.  
2. **Dry Integration**: Whisk flour, sugar, cocoa, baking powder, baking soda, and salt in a large bowl. Ensures even leavening distribution.  
3. **Wet Integration**: Add eggs, buttermilk, oil, and 15ml vanilla extract. Mix on low speed (or by hand) until *just* combined.  
4. **The Bloom**: Carefully pour in the boiling water/coffee. Mix until smooth. The batter will be unnaturally thin. This is correct. Do not add flour.  
5. **Bake**: Divide batter evenly between pans. Bake 30–35 minutes. Test with a toothpick; it should emerge with moist crumbs, not wet batter.  
6. **Cool**: Rest in pans for 10 minutes, then invert onto a wire rack. Never frost a warm cake.  

**Failure Points to Avoid**  
- **Substituting butter for oil**: Butter solidifies at room temperature, creating a drier crumb. Oil remains liquid, guaranteeing moisture.  
- **Overmixing**: Agitating the batter after adding flour develops gluten. Gluten equals toughness.  
- **Skipping the hot liquid**: Un-bloomed cocoa tastes flat and dusty.  
- **Using old leavening agents**: If your baking soda is >6 months old, the cake will not rise. Test it.
